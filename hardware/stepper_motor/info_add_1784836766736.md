# Stepper motor pulse planning

`stepper_motor.h` provides three platform-independent operations:

- `CalculateAction()` converts a relative angular move into acceleration, constant-speed and deceleration pulse series. Speeds are in degrees per second, acceleration is in degrees per second squared, frequencies are in pulses per second, and intervals are in seconds.
- `evaluateSeries()` calculates a series duration, angular displacement and final speed without executing pulses.
- `action()` executes one series. Hardware-specific GPIO behavior is supplied as a callback that emits one pulse and receives the direction (`-1` or `+1`).

`degreesPerPulse` is required because it depends on the motor step angle and the driver microstep configuration.

A pulse series contains the requested pulse count, the interval before the first pulse, and the arithmetic interval change for every following pulse. 

The requested angle is quantized to the nearest whole pulse. `CalculateAction()` rejects a move that starts in the opposite direction; braking through zero and reversing must be planned as separate actions.

The helper implementation uses the kinematic distance `abs(v1²-v0²) / (2 * accelMax)` to choose the pulse count for the fastest speed-change series. Because pulse intervals are stored as an arithmetic progression, the resulting pulse train is an approximation of constant angular acceleration rather than an exact constant-acceleration trajectory.

`getAcceleratedSequence()` fills a remaining displacement with a symmetric acceleration/cruise/deceleration sequence that starts and ends at the supplied speed. The total displacement is quantized to whole pulses.
